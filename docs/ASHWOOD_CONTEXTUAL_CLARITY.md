# ASHWOOD — Contextual Clarity

Status: approved site-wide editorial and interaction principle.

## Core idea

ASHWOOD should take complex ideas, use context to determine what actually matters to the visitor, and articulate that meaning as concisely and clearly as possible.

Complexity may exist in the work. Complexity should not automatically appear in the interface.

The site is not a repository dump, architecture diagram, founder notebook, or product glossary presented all at once. It is an editorial layer that decides what a person needs to understand next.

## Primary rule

**Context first. Compression second. Depth on demand.**

Every public surface should answer:

1. Who is here / what are they trying to understand?
2. What is the smallest useful idea they need now?
3. What evidence, visual, example, or action can communicate it faster than another paragraph?
4. What deeper explanation can remain available without occupying the first reading layer?

## Default hierarchy

A visitor should usually encounter information in this order:

**one clear idea → one concrete example or piece of evidence → one next action → optional depth**

Do not present every relevant concept at the same visual weight.

## Writing rules

- Prefer one strong sentence over a paragraph when the paragraph does not change the decision.
- Do not explain the ecosystem repeatedly across pages.
- Do not define jargon before the visitor needs it.
- When jargon is necessary, translate it inline in plain language.
- Use questions when a question communicates the product/problem faster than a category description.
- Prefer evidence, artifacts, diagrams, screenshots, status, and examples over explanatory prose.
- Preserve nuance in deeper layers; do not destroy nuance merely to sound simple.
- Progressive disclosure is preferred to long landing-page copy.
- A section should have one primary job.
- A page without a distinct job should be merged, redirected, or removed.

## Visual rule

Visual hierarchy should perform part of the explanation.

Use space, scale, sequence, imagery, evidence, state, and interaction to reduce the number of words required. Decorative complexity that makes comprehension harder is a failure even if it looks sophisticated.

## ASHWOOD surface roles

- **Home** — establish point of view and route the visitor.
- **Portfolio** — show the creative/professional work; do not over-explain it.
- **Build Journal** — show the questions, evidence, decisions, and what changed.
- **AI from Zero** — translate unfamiliar AI concepts into useful mental models and next actions.
- **About** — explain the person and point of view only where needed.
- **Workspace** — private owner command center; compress portfolio complexity into what matters, what agents are doing, what needs the owner, and what changed.
- **Product sites** — sell/explain the product itself. ASHWOOD should not duplicate their full product copy.

## AI from Zero application

AI from Zero should not try to teach the entire AI stack on one page.

The landing path should be:

1. **Start with something you already do.**
2. **Use AI for the lightest useful job.** — understand, compare, organize, create, monitor.
3. **Know when an agent is actually useful.** — repeated bounded work, not every task.
4. **Keep judgment with the human.** — context can be shared; authority should be deliberate.
5. **Learn one concept when you need it.** — deeper glossary/reference material is downstream.
6. **Try a concrete tool.** — Build Gate or another interactive learning surface.

The ecosystem is a case study, not required vocabulary.

## Site-wide simplification pass

Future reviews should identify:

- repeated explanations
- sections with multiple competing jobs
- pages that duplicate other pages
- dense card grids where sequence would be clearer
- paragraphs that could become a visual/example
- jargon introduced before need
- architecture labels presented as visitor-facing meaning
- CTAs that compete with each other
- stale information that adds cognitive load

Simplification is not a redesign loop. Preserve working visual identity and behavior unless the current design itself is causing comprehension problems.

## Acceptance test

For each major page, a first-time visitor should be able to answer within roughly ten seconds:

- What is this?
- Why might I care?
- What should I look at or do next?

If the page requires reading several paragraphs before those answers become clear, simplify it.
