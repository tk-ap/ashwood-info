What ties the wall together

The face

One character recurs across the body of work: jagged hairline, red eyes, heavy features. He appears as the ADHOCRACY card inside the Bottle Cap Collector, again monumental — filling the whole red painting — and echoed in the yellow totem's crown and eyes.

Handwriting as a building material

In three pieces the text is not a caption, it is the structure: Love Is Not Poison (the poem is the field the figure lives in), the DUALITY panel inside the Collector (a wall-to-wall argument in several orientations), and the Father Time letter (the letter is the entire image).

Damage & accident, kept visible

The heart's glue seams and missing piece. The epoxy spill that weathered the Collector's glass. The blank page cut open into a window. In every case the accident is kept, not repaired away — the damage stays as evidence of the object's history.

Two families, one crossing

Paintings — bold impasto: faces, flowers, symbols. Assemblage — the Collector: found and given materials (caps, corks, once-live flowers) plus handwriting, sealed and still accumulating. Love Is Not Poison and the heart sit exactly where the two families cross.
