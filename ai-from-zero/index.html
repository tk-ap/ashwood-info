ASHWOOD AI, FROM ZERO

START HERE / NO AI BACKGROUND REQUIRED

You already have an entry point for AI. It is whatever you are already doing.

You do not need to become an AI expert or build an agent. Start with your actual life, work, business, craft, community, or goal. AI can help you think, organize, create, compare, remember, and repeat useful work long before automation becomes necessary.

Find your entry point ↓
01AI CAN DO MORE THAN CHAT

Start with your life, not the technology.

You already hand work to people, calendars, search engines, software, and systems. AI is a new kind of resource: software that can work with language and information, reason through steps, and increasingly use tools on your behalf.

UNDERSTAND

“Explain this letter to me in plain English.”

COMPARE

“Research three options and show me the tradeoffs.”

ORGANIZE

“Turn these scattered notes into a plan I can follow.”

MONITOR

“Watch this project and tell me when something important changes.”

CARRY WORK

“Take this repeatable process through the next steps, and stop when you need my decision.”

Not every AI tool can do every one of these things. What it can actually do depends on the model, the tools it can access, the information you give it, and the permissions you allow.

01AYOU ALREADY HAVE AN ENTRY POINT

You do not need an “AI use case.” Bring the thing you already care about.

The most useful first question is not “How should I use AI?” It is “What am I already trying to understand, improve, make, grow, organize, or decide?” Start there. The complexity can come later — if it earns its way in.

STREETWEAR / SMALL BRAND

Make the brand clearer.

Bring collection ideas, references, customer questions, pricing, and past launches. Ask AI to organize a drop, sharpen creative direction, draft a shoot brief, compare suppliers, or turn scattered ideas into a launch plan.

ATHLETE / BODYBUILDING

See the pattern in the work.

Bring training logs, posing notes, competition dates, goals, and coach feedback. Use AI to organize progress, prepare questions, plan content, or turn notes into a checklist. It can support understanding without replacing qualified health or coaching judgment.

JEWELRY / RETAIL

Turn business information into decisions.

Bring best sellers, margins, inventory, customer questions, vendor notes, and your desired positioning. Ask what patterns are visible, what deserves restocking, how to merchandise a collection, or how to prepare for a pop-up.

PASTOR / COMMUNITY LEADER

Organize what you already carry.

Bring sermon notes, scripture references, event details, community concerns, and prior teaching. Use AI to organize themes, create discussion questions, adapt communications, or prepare study material while keeping theology, care, and judgment human-owned.

CONTENT CREATOR

Learn from your own body of work.

Bring posts, comments, scripts, audience questions, analytics, and ideas. Ask what themes keep resonating, turn one idea into several formats, organize a content series, research a topic, or prepare a sponsor brief.

ANYONE ELSE

Start with the burden you already feel.

Work, school, caregiving, job searching, travel, household planning, creative practice, paperwork, research, decisions — if it involves thinking, language, information, comparison, remembering, or repeated steps, there is probably a useful AI entry point.

The literacy ladder: AI helps me think → AI helps me organize → AI helps me produce → AI helps me repeat a process → only then, when useful, AI coordinates tools or agents.

05Automate only if earned

Turn repeated, bounded work into a workflow or agent when that actually reduces burden.

The goal is not to push everyone toward agentic workflows. A better conversation, clearer plan, stronger draft, useful comparison, or well-organized set of notes may be the entire win.

02WHAT IS AN AGENT?

An answer is not the same as a goal.

“Agent” is still a loose industry term. The useful distinction is simple: some AI mostly responds to you; agentic AI can be given a goal and carry multiple steps toward it, often using tools along the way.

AI ASSISTANT

Helps you think or create.

You ask. It responds. You decide what happens next.

Example: “Tell me how to plan a trip to Kingston.”

AI AGENT

Can carry a goal through steps.

You give it an outcome, context, boundaries, and tools. It works through parts of the process and returns when it needs you.

Example: “Compare flights that fit my dates and budget, prepare an itinerary, and ask me before anything is purchased.”

03WHAT SHOULD I HAND OFF?

The real skill is deciding what stays with you.

AI is most useful when delegation is intentional. You do not have to automate your life. Separate what requires your judgment from what can be shared or carried by a machine.

KEEP

Human judgment.

  • Values and priorities
  • Consent and final decisions
  • Relationships and trust
  • Accountability
  • What “good” actually means
SHARE

Useful context.

  • Your goals
  • Preferences
  • Constraints
  • Relevant history
  • Examples of what you mean
HAND OFF

Work that can be carried.

  • Research and comparison
  • Drafting and organizing
  • Monitoring and reminders
  • Repeatable steps
  • Routine follow-through
04DO I EVEN NEED AN AGENT?

Sometimes a good answer is enough.

Do not add automation just because it exists. Start with the kind of burden you are trying to remove, then choose the lightest useful form of AI.

WHAT IS TAKING YOUR ATTENTION?

Choose the closest version.

Start with the problem, not the word “agent.”

Pick one above. The goal is to see whether you need an answer, structured help, ongoing monitoring, or a bounded workflow.

05WHY CONTEXT + CONTROL MATTER

More capable machines need clearer boundaries.

The more work AI can carry, the more important it becomes to give it the right information, decide what deserves action, constrain what it may do, and verify the result.

Once machines can do more than answer questions, you need better ways to tell them what matters, decide what deserves action, and control what they are allowed to do.

08TRY YOUR FIRST HANDOFF

Start with one thing that keeps taking your attention.

Do not begin with “How do I automate my life?” Begin with a small, repeated burden and describe the handoff clearly enough that both you and the machine know where your responsibility starts again.

I keep spending attention on…

Fill in the template. You can paste it into ChatGPT or another AI tool and use the response to learn what should remain a simple conversation versus what might eventually become a repeatable or agentic workflow.

A useful first handoff has an outcome, enough context, a permission boundary, and a definition of done.

I want AI to help me with: ________

The outcome I want is: ________

Useful context: ________

It may do this without asking me: ________

It must ask me before: ________

I will consider this done when: ________
